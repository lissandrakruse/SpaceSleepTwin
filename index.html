EMPIRICAL RESEARCH ROADMAP

Hypothesis-driven light–sleep modeling

H1: Greater melanopic light exposure during the three hours before bedtime is associated with longer sleep-onset latency, controlling for relevant participant and timing factors. This is a testable observational hypothesis, not an established causal effect of LEDs in spaceflight.

An independent Python analysis pipeline has been added for authorized participant-day observations, grouped train/test splitting, and MAE/RMSE/R² reporting. No empirical cohort has been ingested or validated yet. The phase simulator above remains a theoretical scenario model.

EMPIRICAL DATA WORKSPACE

◈ Real Light & Sleep Observations

Import an authorized, de-identified CSV locally in your browser. No records are uploaded to a server or committed to GitHub. Accepted headers: timestamp,whitelight,wake or linetime,whitelight,wake. The wake column should be coded 1 = wake, 0 = sleep. Values with other encodings require recoding before import.

Scientific foundations & extended bibliography

These references motivate the research questions and modeling choices; they do not establish that this exploratory simulator has been validated.

  1. Brown TM et al. (2022). Recommendations for daytime, evening, and nighttime indoor light exposure to best support physiology, sleep, and wakefulness. PLOS Biology · DOI. Application: exposure timing benchmarks for terrestrial settings.
  2. CIE S 026/E:2018. CIE System for Metrology of Optical Radiation for ipRGC-Influenced Responses to Light. CIE standard. Application: future spectral-to-melanopic calculations.
  3. Jewett ME, Forger DB, Kronauer RE (1999). Revised limit cycle oscillator model of human circadian pacemaker. PubMed. Application: target mechanistic circadian model for future versions; not yet implemented here.
  4. Borbély AA (1982). A two process model of sleep regulation. PubMed. Application: future homeostatic sleep-pressure module.
  5. Phillips AJK et al. Mathematical modeling of circadian rhythms and sleep-wake regulation. PubMed literature search. Application: model development and parameter estimation.
  6. Rahman SA and colleagues. Dynamic lighting and circadian adaptation during a 45-day space analog mission. Full-text research article. Application: space-analog study design and external comparison.
  7. Dynamic lighting schedules to facilitate circadian adaptation to shifted timing of sleep and wake. Full-text research article. Application: LED schedules and circadian adaptation.
  8. NASA Human Research Program. Program overview. Application: operational spaceflight research context.
  9. National Sleep Research Resource. Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) Sleep: actigraphy documentation. Dataset documentation. Application: light/activity/wake time series, subject to data access conditions.
  10. NASA Open Science Data Repository. OSDR data portal. Application: discovery of openly accessible space biology datasets.

Dataset provenance, permissions, and preprocessing must be documented for any published empirical analysis. Observational files are never distributed by this website.
